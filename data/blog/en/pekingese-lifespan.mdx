## How Long Do Pekingese Live? The Short Answer

**The typical Pekingese lifespan is 12 to 14 years.** Both the [AKC's Pekingese breed page](https://www.akc.org/dog-breeds/pekingese/) and [PetMD's Pekingese guide](https://www.petmd.com/dog/breeds/pekingese) give that range.

That's a solid lifespan, in line with many toy breeds. Where a Peke lands within it — or beyond it — depends heavily on how well a few breed-specific issues are managed, especially breathing, heat, weight, and eyes.

## Health Issues That Affect Pekingese Lifespan

PetMD describes the Pekingese as a mostly healthy breed that's predisposed to a few specific conditions. Knowing them helps you catch problems early.

### Brachycephalic airway syndrome

That famous flat face comes with a narrowed airway. [VCA Animal Hospitals](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/brachycephalic-airway-syndrome-in-dogs) explains that short-faced breeds can have narrow nostrils, an elongated soft palate, and other structural changes that make breathing harder.

Signs to watch: loud snoring, noisy breathing, gagging, and tiring quickly on walks. **Mild cases can be managed at home, while more severe cases may benefit from surgery.** Your vet can tell you where your dog falls.

### Heat sensitivity

Because they can't pant as efficiently, PetMD notes that Pekingese are **more prone to overheating**. Their thick double coat doesn't help. Heatstroke is a real emergency for this breed, so plan walks for cool mornings and evenings.

### Eye problems

Those large, prominent eyes are more exposed than in longer-nosed breeds. PetMD lists dry eye (keratoconjunctivitis sicca), where the eye doesn't make enough tears, as a common Peke concern. Squinting, redness, thick discharge, or pawing at the face all deserve a prompt vet visit.

### Patellar luxation

In this condition, the kneecap slips out of place. You may see your Peke skip or hop on three legs for a few steps. Mild cases may need only monitoring, while others need treatment to prevent arthritis.

### Weight gain

Pekingese aren't high-energy dogs, and a few extra pounds on a 14-pound frame is a big percentage. Extra weight makes breathing harder and adds strain to joints and the back.

## What This Means for Your Pekingese

Pekes are companion dogs through and through. Their lifespan depends less on hard exercise and more on protecting their body from the stresses it handles poorly.

### Puppy and adult years (0–8)

Focus on building habits: a lean body, a harness-trained walker, and comfortable handling of the face, eyes, and mouth. This is also the time to learn your dog's normal breathing sounds so you notice changes later.

### Senior years (around 8–10 and up)

Small breeds are often considered seniors around 8 to 10 years. Move to twice-yearly checkups and watch for slowing down, cloudy eyes, dental pain, or new breathing noise. Our [senior dog food guide](/blog/senior-dog-food) covers diet changes that may help.

## Practical Ways to Help Your Pekingese Live Longer

1. **Keep them lean.** Measure meals and count treats. You should be able to feel the ribs under the coat.
2. **Beat the heat.** Walk in cool hours, provide shade and water, and never leave them in a car.
3. **Use a harness, not a collar.** A harness takes pressure off the neck and airway.
4. **Protect the eyes.** Wipe gently around them daily and see the vet quickly for any redness or squinting.
5. **Brush the coat regularly.** Mats trap heat and hide skin problems.
6. **Stay on top of dental care.** Small mouths crowd teeth, which makes dental disease more likely. Brushing and vet cleanings help.
7. **Keep exercise gentle and daily.** Short walks and indoor play keep muscles and joints strong without overheating.

## When to Call Your Vet

Pekes are stoic little dogs, and flat-faced breeds can go downhill quickly when breathing or heat is involved. Call your vet promptly if you notice:

- Heavy, noisy panting that doesn't settle after rest, or gums that look bright red, pale, or bluish (treat as an emergency in hot weather)
- Snoring or breathing noise that is new or getting louder
- A bulging, red, cloudy, or squinting eye
- Limping, skipping, or reluctance to jump or use stairs
- Yelping when picked up, a hunched posture, or wobbly back legs
- Weight loss, bad breath, or trouble chewing

Catching these changes early is one of the simplest ways to protect your Peke's lifespan. Our guide on [how to tell if your dog is sick](/blog/how-can-you-tell-if-your-dog-is-sick) covers more general warning signs.

## The Bottom Line

Pekingese typically live 12 to 14 years, and many reach the top of that range with good care. Keep your Peke cool, lean, and comfortable, stay alert to breathing and eye changes, and book regular vet visits as they age.
