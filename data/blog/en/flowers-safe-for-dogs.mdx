## Which Flowers Are Safe for Dogs? The Short Answer

**Plenty of popular flowers are safe for dogs, including roses, sunflowers, snapdragons, petunias, zinnias, orchids, and gerbera daisies.** You can plant a colorful, dog-friendly garden or keep a bouquet on the table without worry.

"Safe" here means non-toxic. The [ASPCA's plant list for dogs](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/animal-poison-control/dogs-plant-list) is the best reference, and it notes that eating any plant material can still cause a little vomiting or diarrhea. So the goal is a garden that won't poison your dog, not a salad bar.

## 14 Flowers That Are Safe for Dogs

All of the flowers below are listed as non-toxic to dogs by the ASPCA.

### Garden favorites

1. **Roses** — Non-toxic, but watch the thorns. They can scratch noses, mouths, and paws.
2. **Sunflowers** — Cheerful, tall, and safe. The ASPCA says they're unlikely to cause more than mild stomach upset.
3. **Snapdragons** — Safe, though the bitter taste can make a dog drool for a bit.
4. **Petunias** — A great choice for pots, hanging baskets, and borders.
5. **Zinnias** — Easy to grow, long-blooming, and pet-friendly.
6. **Gerbera daisies** — Bright and safe. Note that not every "daisy" is safe; chrysanthemums are toxic.
7. **Camellias** — A lovely shrub for shade gardens.

### Houseplants and bouquet flowers

8. **Orchids** — The ASPCA's [guide to pet-safe bouquets](https://www.aspca.org/news/mothers-day-bouquets-whats-safe-pets) lists Phalaenopsis, Dendrobium, Oncidium, and Cymbidium orchids as safe.
9. **African violets** — A classic windowsill plant that's non-toxic.
10. **Freesias** — Common bouquet fillers, non-toxic apart from possible stomach upset.
11. **Lisianthus** — Rose-like blooms that last up to two weeks in a vase.
12. **Statice (Limonium)** — A safe filler flower.
13. **Stock** — Fragrant and safe for bouquets.
14. **Madagascar jasmine** — May cause mild stomach upset but no other known risk.

## Common Flowers to Keep Away From Dogs

Some of the most popular garden and holiday flowers are on the ASPCA's toxic list for dogs. The [AKC's guide to poisonous plants](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/home-living/poisonous-plants-for-dogs/) covers many of them in detail.

| Flower             | Why it's a concern                              |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Azalea             | Can cause vomiting, drooling, and heart effects |
| Daffodil           | Bulbs are the most toxic part                   |
| Tulip              | Bulbs are the most toxic part                   |
| Oleander           | Contains compounds that affect the heart        |
| Foxglove           | Contains compounds that affect the heart        |
| Lily of the valley | Contains compounds that affect the heart        |
| Autumn crocus      | Can cause severe illness                        |
| Chrysanthemum      | Can cause stomach upset and drooling            |

**Bulbs deserve extra caution.** Dogs that dig up freshly planted tulip or daffodil bulbs get a far more concentrated dose than one nibbling a petal. For a longer list, see our guide to [poisonous plants for dogs](/blog/poisonous-plants-for-dogs).

## Tips for a Dog-Friendly Garden

1. **Check before you buy.** Look up any new plant on the ASPCA list. Common names can be confusing, so use the scientific name when you can.
2. **Keep bouquets up high.** Even safe flowers can cause stomach upset, and long stems can be a choking or blockage risk for big chewers.
3. **Watch the extras.** Fertilizers, pesticides, snail bait, and cocoa mulch can be more dangerous than the plants themselves.
4. **Fence off bulb beds** while bulbs are freshly planted and the soil smells interesting.
5. **Give your dog their own space.** A digging pit or sniffing corner can steer them away from your flower beds.
6. **Train "leave it."** It's the single most useful cue for a garden-loving dog. Our [dog training tips](/blog/dog-training-tips) walk through the basics.

Houseplants count too. Our guide to the [snake plant](/blog/snake-plant-poisonous-to-dogs) and to [household items dangerous to dogs](/blog/household-items-dangerous-to-dogs) cover indoor risks.

## Signs Your Dog Ate Something It Shouldn't

Mild stomach upset after eating a safe plant usually passes within a day. Watch for:

- Vomiting or diarrhea
- Drooling or pawing at the mouth
- Loss of appetite
- Lethargy or weakness
- Tremors, a slow or irregular heartbeat, or collapse (seek help immediately)

## When to Call Your Vet

Call your vet or the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center at (888) 426-4435 (a consultation fee may apply) if:

- Your dog ate any part of a plant on the toxic list
- You don't know what they ate
- Symptoms are more than mild or last longer than a day
- Your dog dug up and chewed bulbs

Take a photo of the plant or bring a piece with you. It helps your vet act fast.

## The Bottom Line

You don't have to choose between a beautiful garden and a safe dog. Stick with pet-friendly flowers like roses, sunflowers, zinnias, petunias, and orchids, skip the toxic favorites, and keep bouquets and bulbs out of reach.
