## What Is a Labrador Retriever? The Short Answer

**The Labrador Retriever is a friendly, athletic, medium-to-large sporting breed originally developed to retrieve for hunters, and it is one of the most loved family dogs in the world.** Labs are known for their even temper, love of food, and bottomless enthusiasm for anything involving water or a ball.

The Lab held the top spot in the AKC's popularity rankings for 31 years before the French Bulldog took over in 2022. According to the [AKC's 2025 rankings](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/dog-breeds/most-popular-dog-breeds-2025/), Labs are still firmly at No. 2.

Here is what you should know before bringing one home, or to take even better care of the one snoring on your couch.

## Where Labs Come From

Despite the name, the breed's roots are in Newfoundland, Canada, where water dogs helped fishermen haul nets and fetch fish. British breeders refined the dogs in the 1800s into the retriever we know today. The [AKC's history of the Labrador](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/dog-breeds/labrador-retriever-history/) covers the full story.

That working background explains a lot about modern Labs:

- They love to carry things in their mouths
- Most are natural swimmers with a water-resistant coat and an "otter" tail
- They were bred to work closely with people, so they crave company and a job

### Show vs field Labs

You'll often hear about "English" (show) and "American" (field) Labs. They are the same breed, but show lines tend to be stockier with broader heads, while field lines are leaner and often more intense. Energy levels can differ quite a bit between the two.

## Temperament: What Living With a Lab Is Like

Labs are famously good-natured. They tend to get along with kids, strangers, and other pets, which is why they're so popular as family dogs and assistance dogs.

That friendliness comes with a few realities:

- **They're not guard dogs.** Most Labs would rather greet a burglar than stop one.
- **They're mouthy.** Puppies especially love to chew, so plan for plenty of safe chew toys.
- **They're food-obsessed.** This makes training easy and weight control harder.
- **They don't like being alone for long.** A bored, lonely Lab often turns into a destructive one.

Labs are very trainable with reward-based methods. Start early with basics and socialization. Our guides to [puppy training tips](/blog/puppy-training-tips) and [how to train your dog](/blog/how-to-train-your-dog) are a good place to begin.

## Labrador Health and Lifespan

Labs are generally sturdy, but some health issues come up again and again.

The best data comes from a [2018 study of more than 33,000 Labradors in UK vet clinics](https://cgejournal.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s40575-018-0064-x), published in _Canine Genetics and Epidemiology_. It found:

- A **median lifespan of about 12 years**
- Chocolate Labs lived shorter lives on average (about 10.7 years) than black and yellow Labs (about 12.1 years)
- The most common problems were **ear infections, obesity, and degenerative joint disease**

### Common health issues to watch for

1. **Ear infections.** Floppy ears plus a love of water trap moisture. Dry ears after swimming and check them weekly.
2. **Obesity.** Many Labs carry a gene variant linked to stronger hunger, so they tend to beg and gain weight easily. Our guide to [Labrador Retriever food](/blog/labrador-retriever-food) explains this and how to portion meals.
3. **Hip and elbow dysplasia.** These inherited joint problems can cause lameness and arthritis. [VCA's overview of hip dysplasia](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/hip-dysplasia-in-dogs) explains the signs and treatment options.
4. **Arthritis.** Common in older Labs, especially overweight ones.
5. **Inherited eye and muscle conditions.** Responsible breeders screen for several of these with DNA and eye exams.

**If you're buying a puppy**, ask the breeder for hip and elbow evaluations on both parents, plus eye exams and DNA test results. A good breeder will be happy to share them.

## Daily Care for a Happy Lab

### Exercise

Labs are athletes. Many adult Labs do best with an hour or more of activity a day, split between walks, fetch, swimming, and training games. Young Labs often need more. Without it, they tend to find their own entertainment, like chewing the couch.

For puppies, keep exercise short and gentle while their joints are still growing.

### Mental stimulation

A tired body is only half the equation. Food puzzles, scent games, and short training sessions help burn off mental energy. Our list of [games for dogs](/blog/games-for-dogs) has easy ideas.

### Grooming

The short double coat is low-maintenance but sheds a lot, especially during seasonal coat "blows." Brush once or twice a week (daily during heavy shedding), trim nails regularly, and brush teeth several times a week.

### Nutrition and weight

Feed a complete and balanced food, measure every meal, and count treats. Large-breed puppy food is important for Lab puppies to support steady, healthy growth. You should always be able to feel your Lab's ribs without pressing hard.

Keeping a Lab lean is one of the most powerful things you can do for them. A landmark lifetime study of Labs found that those fed 25% less than their littermates lived a median of about 1.8 years longer. Our guide to [how long dogs live](/blog/how-long-do-dogs-live) explains more about what drives lifespan.

## Is a Labrador Retriever Right for You?

A Lab could be a great fit if you:

- Have an active lifestyle and time for daily exercise
- Want a friendly, social dog that's great with family and guests
- Enjoy training and don't mind some fur on everything

A Lab may not be ideal if you:

- Are away from home most of the day with no help
- Want a low-shedding or low-energy dog
- Live somewhere without easy access to space for exercise

## Practical Takeaways

- Labs are friendly, trainable, and high-energy, especially in the first few years
- Expect 55 to 80 pounds and a lifespan of around 11 to 13 years
- Ear infections, obesity, and joint problems are the most common health issues
- Keeping your Lab lean is the single biggest thing you can do for their long-term health
- Choose a breeder who health-tests both parents
