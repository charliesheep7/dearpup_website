## The Short Answer

**Yes, puppies can eat eggs — as long as they are fully cooked, plain, and given in small amounts.** Eggs are a good source of protein and healthy fats, and most puppies love them.

The catch is that a puppy's main job is growing, and growth depends on a carefully balanced diet. Eggs are a bonus on top of complete puppy food, not a replacement for it.

## Why Eggs Can Be Good for Puppies

The [AKC's guide to eggs](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/nutrition/can-dogs-eat-eggs/) explains that when eggs are fully cooked and fed in moderation, they provide **protein, healthy fats, vitamins, and minerals** that support overall health.

For puppies, that can mean:

- **A high-value training treat.** Tiny bits of cooked egg work well for early training sessions.
- **An appetite boost.** A little egg on top of kibble can tempt a picky eater.
- **Easy digestion.** Plain cooked egg is gentle for most young stomachs.

[PetMD](https://www.petmd.com/dog/nutrition/can-dogs-eat-eggs) agrees that boiled, scrambled, or poached eggs are safe, while raw or undercooked eggs should be avoided.

## Why Puppies Need Extra Care

### Puppy food is already balanced

A good puppy food is formulated to deliver exactly the right mix of calories, protein, calcium, and phosphorus for growth. Every extra food you add shifts that balance a little.

That is fine in small amounts. But if eggs start replacing meals, the diet can drift off track — especially for large-breed puppies, whose growing bones are sensitive to nutrient balance. Our guide to [food for large-breed puppies](/blog/puppy-food-for-large-breed-puppies) explains why.

### Eggs are calorie-dense

The AKC notes that because eggs are calorie-dense, feeding too many can contribute to weight gain or unbalance an already complete diet. Puppies should grow lean and steady, not fast and chubby.

### Young tummies are sensitive

Any new food can cause loose stool in a puppy. Introduce egg slowly so you can tell whether it agrees with your pup.

## How to Serve Eggs to a Puppy

1. **Cook it fully.** Scrambled, hard-boiled, or poached — the white and yolk should both be set.
2. **Keep it plain.** No salt, butter, oil, milk, cheese, or seasoning. Onion and garlic are toxic to dogs, so skip leftover omelets.
3. **Let it cool** and cut it into small pieces.
4. **Start tiny.** Offer a small taste and watch for 24 hours.
5. **Keep it occasional.** A few times a week at most.

### How much egg is okay?

A rough starting point:

| Puppy size             | Serving size                    |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Toy and small breeds   | A teaspoon or two of cooked egg |
| Medium breeds          | About a quarter of an egg       |
| Large and giant breeds | Up to half an egg               |

PetMD recommends that **all treats combined make up no more than 10% of your dog's diet**. Eggs count toward that total. Our [puppy feeding chart](/blog/how-much-to-feed-a-puppy-chart) helps you see what your puppy's full day should look like.

## Raw Eggs and Eggshells: Skip Them

### Raw eggs

Both the AKC and PetMD advise against raw eggs. The two main concerns:

- **Salmonella.** Raw eggs can carry bacteria that cause vomiting, diarrhea, and dehydration — and can spread to the people in your home.
- **Biotin absorption.** Raw egg whites contain avidin, which binds biotin, a B vitamin important for skin and coat.

Cooking removes both problems. There is no nutritional reason to feed a puppy raw egg. For more, see our guide to [raw eggs for dogs](/blog/can-dogs-eat-raw-eggs).

### Eggshells

Ground eggshell is sometimes used as a calcium supplement for adult dogs. For puppies, it is best avoided unless your vet specifically recommends it. Complete puppy food already contains the calcium your puppy needs, and adding more can upset the balance growing bones depend on. Our [eggshells guide](/blog/can-dogs-eat-egg-shells) covers adult use.

## Signs Egg Doesn't Agree With Your Puppy

Most puppies handle cooked egg well. Stop feeding it and watch closely if you see:

- Loose stool or diarrhea
- Vomiting
- Gas or a gurgly stomach
- Itching, red skin, or ear scratching — possible signs of a food allergy

Egg allergies are uncommon but do happen. If symptoms show up every time you give egg, leave it out.

## When to Call Your Vet

Puppies have little reserve, and they can get dehydrated quickly. Call your vet if your puppy:

- Vomits more than once or has diarrhea lasting beyond a day
- Seems lethargic or refuses food
- Ate a large amount of raw egg and shows any stomach upset
- Shows swelling of the face or trouble breathing (rare, but urgent)

When your puppy is young, a quick call is always the right move.

## The Bottom Line

Puppies can enjoy eggs as long as they are fully cooked, plain, and kept small. Use them as a training treat or a topper, not a meal replacement, and skip raw eggs and eggshells.
