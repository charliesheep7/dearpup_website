## The Short Answer

There's no single number that fits every puppy — the right amount depends on their age, expected adult size, and the specific food's calories. The reliable method is a three-step one: **start with the chart on your food bag, split it into the right number of meals for your puppy's age, then adjust to your puppy's body condition.**

Below you'll find a meal-schedule chart, a switch-to-adult-food chart, and how to fine-tune portions so your puppy grows at a healthy, steady pace instead of too fast.

## Start With the Bag, Then Adjust

Every quality puppy food prints a feeding chart, usually organized by the puppy's **expected adult weight** and current age. That's your starting point — but it's only a starting point.

[PetMD](https://www.petmd.com/dog/nutrition/how-much-to-feed-puppy) notes that bag charts often don't nail the exact amount for an individual puppy, and that a puppy's real needs can vary up to 50% in either direction based on body condition. Calorie needs also drop as puppies mature: a 10-pound puppy needs far more energy under 4 months than the same weight puppy over 4 months.

So treat the chart as version one. Feed that amount for a week or two, watch your puppy's shape, and adjust up or down from there.

## Puppy Feeding Schedule by Age

How often you feed matters as much as how much. Young puppies have tiny stomachs and high energy needs, so they eat more frequently and taper off with age. Here's the general schedule the [AKC](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/nutrition/puppy-feeding-fundamentals/) and PetMD recommend:

| Puppy age | Meals per day | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Under 3 months | 3–4 | Toy breeds may need 4–6 small meals |
| 3–6 months | 3 | Drop from four to three during this window |
| 6–12 months | 2 | Move to twice daily |
| Adult (1 year+) | 2 | Most dogs stay on two meals for life |

Divide the total daily amount evenly across those meals. Feeding at **regular times in measured amounts** — and picking up the bowl after about 15 minutes — keeps digestion predictable and discourages picky habits.

## Measure Meals — Don't Free-Feed

It's tempting to leave a full bowl out all day, but for a growing puppy, **measured meals are the better method.** Free-feeding makes it nearly impossible to track how much your puppy actually eats, encourages grazing and pickiness, and quietly leads to overfeeding.

Use an actual measuring cup or a kitchen scale, not a guess. Precision now sets up a lifetime of easy portion control. Our guide on [how much to feed a dog](/blog/how-much-food-should-i-feed-my-dog) covers the same principle for the adult years.

## The Large-Breed Exception

If you have a large or giant-breed puppy, growth speed matters even more than portion size. Feeding too much — or the wrong formula — can make big puppies grow **too fast**, which stresses developing joints and raises the risk of orthopedic problems.

Large-breed puppy foods are formulated with controlled calcium and calories to promote slower, steadier growth. Feed a large-breed-specific puppy formula until your dog matures, and be conservative with portions. Our overview of [large-breed dog food](/blog/large-breed-dog-food) explains what to look for. For food type and texture in the early months, see our guides to [wet puppy food](/blog/wet-puppy-food) and [grain-free puppy food](/blog/grain-free-puppy-food).

## When to Switch to Adult Food

Puppies need puppy food until they've finished most of their growth. The timing scales with size:

| Adult size | Weight | Switch around |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small | under 25 lbs | 10–12 months |
| Medium | 25–50 lbs | 12–15 months |
| Large / giant | 50+ lbs | 15–24 months |

Make the change gradually over about a week, mixing increasing amounts of adult food into the puppy food to avoid stomach upset.

## Check Body Condition, Not Just the Scale

The best portion check isn't a number — it's your hands. You should be able to **feel your puppy's ribs with a light touch** (not see them jutting out) and see a visible waist when you look down from above.

A round, chubby puppy isn't a healthy goal; the aim is steady, moderate growth. If your puppy is filling out too fast or looking too lean, adjust the amount and check in with your vet. When in doubt, a quick body-condition check beats trusting any chart on its own.
