## The Short Answer

**Some dogs can eat a small piece of plain mozzarella occasionally.** It is still a fatty dairy treat, so skip it if your dog reacts to dairy or has a history of pancreatitis. Check the label for garlic, onion, or other added seasonings, and avoid using pizza as a cheese treat.

The [American Kennel Club](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/nutrition/can-dogs-eat-cheese/) lists mozzarella among its lower-fat cheese options. That does not make it suitable for every dog: some dogs react to dairy, and a large fatty serving can be risky. A few shreds may work as a training reward, but cheese should remain an occasional treat.

## Why Mozzarella Is One of the Safer Cheeses

Cheese gets a mixed reputation with dogs, and much of that comes down to two things: **fat and lactose**. Mozzarella is often offered as a lower-fat cheese option, but fat content varies by product and dogs differ in how they tolerate dairy.

- **Fat still matters.** Check the label and keep portions small. Fatty treats can upset a dog's stomach and may trigger pancreatitis in a susceptible dog.
- **Dairy tolerance** varies by dog. Cheese generally contains less lactose than milk, but even a small amount can upset a dog with a strong sensitivity.

If you want the full rundown on the whole family, our guide on [whether dogs can eat cheese](/blog/can-dogs-eat-cheese) breaks it down cheese by cheese.

## How Much Mozzarella Is Safe

Portion is everything. The [World Small Animal Veterinary Association](https://wsava.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Feeding-treats-to-your-dog-v2.pdf) advises keeping all treats to **no more than 10% of your dog's daily calories**. Cheese can add up quickly within that allowance.

A few shreds or one small cube is enough for many dogs; offer less to a smaller dog. If your dog needs a calorie-controlled diet or has a medical condition, ask your vet about treats before offering cheese.

Only offer **plain** mozzarella. Check the ingredients before sharing any flavored, shredded, or prepared cheese; avoid products containing garlic or onion.

## When Mozzarella Becomes a Problem

Mozzarella is an occasional option only when plain and served in a small amount. Prepared dishes and added ingredients change the risk:

- **Pizza mozzarella.** Pizza recipes may include onion, garlic, fatty toppings, and plenty of salt. Do not treat a slice of pizza as a safe cheese serving. The risk also applies to other dishes such as [mac and cheese](/blog/can-dogs-eat-mac-and-cheese), where the added ingredients matter.
- **String cheese.** A small piece of plain string cheese may be tolerated, but check its label for salt and seasonings. Treat it as an occasional nibble.
- **Too much, too often.** Cheese adds calories, and frequent servings can contribute to weight gain.
- **Dairy-sensitive dogs.** Dairy is one of the more common food allergens in dogs. According to [VCA Animal Hospitals](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/food-allergies-in-dogs), proteins from dairy, beef, chicken, egg, soy, and wheat are the usual triggers. If your dog already reacts to dairy, mozzarella is not the exception — see our guide on [dog food for food allergies](/blog/dog-food-for-food-allergies) for the bigger picture.

## Signs to Watch For

The first time your dog tries mozzarella, start with a crumb and keep an eye out over the next day. Most dogs are fine, but a lactose-sensitive one may show:

- Gas or bloating
- Loose stools or diarrhea
- Vomiting
- General stomach gurgling or discomfort

Stop offering dairy if these signs appear. If symptoms are severe, persistent, or accompanied by pain or lethargy, contact your veterinarian.

## When to Call Your Vet

Reach out to your veterinarian if the symptoms are more than a passing tummy grumble:

- Repeated vomiting or diarrhea, especially if your dog seems weak or cannot keep water down
- Signs of pain, a hunched posture, or refusing to eat (possible pancreatitis, especially after a large or fatty amount)
- Swelling, hives, or trouble breathing; trouble breathing needs urgent veterinary care

And if your dog raids the fridge and eats a big block of mozzarella, don't panic, but do call your vet for advice, especially for small breeds where a large fatty load hits harder.
