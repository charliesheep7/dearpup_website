## The Short Answer

**A 4-year-old dog is about 32 in human years.** Fully grown, fully adult, and in the middle of what is usually the healthiest, most capable stretch of their life.

Four is a quiet age. There is no puppy chaos to manage and no senior care to plan. That is exactly why it is the easiest time to build habits that pay off a decade from now.

## How the Conversion Works

**The veterinary rule of thumb.** The [AKC's guide to dog years](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/health/how-to-calculate-dog-years-to-human-years/) counts the first year of a medium-sized dog's life as about 15 human years, the second as about nine more, and each year after that as roughly four to five. Two more years on top of 24 puts a 4-year-old at **around 32**.

**The DNA clock.** A 2019 UC San Diego study compared age-related DNA changes in Labradors and humans. The resulting formula — 16 times the natural log of the dog's age, plus 31 — gives **about 53** at age four.

That second number sounds too high, and for everyday purposes it is. The formula treats aging as heavily front-loaded, so it runs well above the rule of thumb for young and middle-aged dogs. The two methods only start to agree around 10 to 12. For a 4-year-old, think early 30s.

## Does Size Matter at Four?

A little, and more every year from here. [VCA Animal Hospitals](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/how-old-is-old-comparing-dog-age-to-human-age) explains that larger dogs age faster after maturity, while smaller dogs age more slowly through adulthood.

At four:

- **Toy and small breeds** are in their early 30s with many years ahead.
- **Medium breeds** sit right around 32.
- **Large breeds** are in their mid-30s.
- **Giant breeds** are closer to 40 and roughly halfway through their expected lifespan.

For a Great Dane, four is mid-life. For a Chihuahua, it is barely a third of the way. Our guide to [how long dogs live](/blog/how-long-do-dogs-live) shows the full range by breed size.

## The Three Things That Matter Most at Four

### 1. Weight

This is the big one. [VCA's guide to obesity in dogs](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/obesity-in-dogs) cites a 2022 survey finding that **59% of dogs are overweight**, and notes that being even moderately overweight can cut life expectancy by nearly two years.

Weight gain at four is usually slow and invisible. The portion size set at age one stays the same while activity drifts down. A few extra treats a day do the rest.

Simple fixes:

1. **Measure meals** with a cup or scale rather than eyeballing the bowl.
2. **Count treats** as part of the day — VCA recommends keeping them under 10% of daily calories.
3. **Check body condition** monthly: you should feel ribs easily under a thin layer, and see a waist from above.

Our [dog calorie calculator](/blog/dog-calorie-calculator) is a good place to check what your dog actually needs.

### 2. Teeth

Dental disease builds quietly through adulthood. Plaque hardens into tartar, gums get inflamed, and by the time you notice bad breath, the problem is well established. [VCA's dental disease guide](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/dental-disease-in-dogs) explains how that process works.

Brushing a few times a week is the single most effective home habit. If your dog has never had it done, four is not too late to start — go slow, use dog toothpaste, and keep sessions short.

### 3. Movement and enrichment

Four-year-olds have energy and a fully developed brain. A dog who is under-exercised or bored at this age often shows it through chewing, barking, or restlessness.

The [AKC's exercise guide](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/health/how-much-exercise-does-dog-need/) notes that needs vary widely by breed. A mix of walks, off-leash play where safe, sniffing time, and short training sessions usually covers it. For ideas, see our [games for dogs](/blog/games-for-dogs) roundup.

A useful test: a well-exercised adult dog settles easily at home. If your dog is pacing, pestering, or destroying things in the evening, more activity — physical or mental — is usually the fix, not more rules.

## Health Checks at Four

Your 4-year-old should still be seeing the vet once a year. Use those visits well:

- **Record a healthy weight** so you have a clear number to compare against later.
- **Ask about dental cleaning** if tartar is building up.
- **Keep vaccines and parasite prevention current.**
- **Mention any behavior change.** New grumpiness, reluctance to jump, or licking a paw can all be signs of pain rather than personality.

Many vets also suggest baseline bloodwork in healthy adult dogs, so future results have a normal to compare against.

## Practical Takeaways

Four in dog years is about 32 in human years — fully adult, with most of life still ahead. The habits you keep now decide how the next decade goes:

- Keep your dog lean
- Brush their teeth
- Keep them moving and thinking
- Keep yearly vet visits
