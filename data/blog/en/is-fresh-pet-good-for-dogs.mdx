You've seen it in the refrigerated case at the pet store or the grocery aisle: rolls and bags of fresh, meaty dog food that look more like something you'd cook than pour. Freshpet is the biggest name in that category, and if you've stood there wondering whether it's genuinely better for your dog or just a premium price tag, you're asking the right question.

The honest answer: Freshpet is a legitimately good food for most healthy dogs, with real advantages and a few real trade-offs. Here's what the evidence actually says, minus the marketing gloss.

## The Short Answer

**For many healthy dogs, a Freshpet meal can be a good everyday food if its label says it is complete and balanced for their life stage.** Freshpet says its meals meet AAFCO nutrient profiles, but its treats and Healthy Mixers are supplemental rather than complete diets ([Freshpet's product FAQ](https://www.freshpet.com/contact)). The exact recipe and label matter more than the brand name alone.

That doesn't make it the right pick for every dog or every budget. But "is it good?" and "is it worth it for my dog?" are two different questions, and it helps to separate them.

## What Makes Fresh Refrigerated Food Different

The thing that sets Freshpet apart from a bag of kibble isn't the ingredient list so much as how the food is made and stored.

Kibble is cooked and extruded into dry, shelf-stable pieces. Freshpet's meals are fully cooked and sold refrigerated. The main differences you can see on the label and in daily use are moisture content, calorie density, storage needs, and price; the cooking method alone does not prove one food is healthier.

That processing difference has a few knock-on effects:

- **More moisture.** Fresh food is much higher in water content than dry kibble, which helps with hydration.
- **High palatability.** Dogs tend to love the smell and texture, which is why it's often recommended for picky eaters.
- **Refrigerated storage.** The food is perishable, so it needs a different handling routine from shelf-stable kibble.

Some dogs do well on a fresh diet, but digestion varies by recipe and by dog. A fresh label alone cannot tell you whether a specific food will suit your pet.

## Is It Actually Better Than Kibble?

This is where honesty matters. Fresh food may be _more appealing_ to your dog, but "better" depends on the recipe and your dog's needs.

The American Kennel Club puts it plainly: fresh dog food can be nutritionally equivalent to kibble as long as it comes from a reputable company that relies on a board-certified veterinary nutritionist to develop its recipes ([AKC](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/nutrition/fresh-raw-kibble-dog-food/)). In other words, the format is not magic. A well-made kibble beats a poorly formulated fresh food every time. What you're really paying for with Freshpet is fresh ingredients, high moisture, and minimal processing, not a guaranteed nutritional upgrade.

**A good kibble is still a genuinely good choice for many dogs.** It's affordable, convenient, and easy to store. If your dog is thriving on it, you don't need to switch. Fresh food shines most for dogs who are picky, need more moisture, or simply do better on a less-processed diet.

Whichever format you choose, the more useful habit is checking what's actually in the specific product in front of you rather than trusting the category label.

## The Trade-Offs to Know

No food is perfect, and fresh refrigerated food comes with a few honest downsides.

### Cost

Fresh food often costs more per day than kibble, especially for a large dog. Compare products using each recipe's calories per package and your dog's daily calorie needs, since package size alone can be misleading. Many owners split the difference by using fresh food as a topper over kibble or feeding it for one meal a day.

### Storage and shelf life

Freshpet must be refrigerated, including before opening. Most opened meals need to be used within seven days, but some varieties have shorter windows, so follow the directions on the package ([Freshpet's storage FAQ](https://www.freshpet.com/contact)). That means fridge space, planning, and a bit more waste if you don't get through it in time.

Handling matters too. Freshpet recommends removing uneaten food from the bowl after one hour. The [FDA advises](https://www.fda.gov/animal-veterinary/animal-health-literacy/tips-safe-handling-pet-food-and-treats) washing your hands before and after handling pet food, cleaning bowls after each use, and promptly covering and refrigerating leftovers. These are simple habits, but they are part of the cost of feeding a refrigerated food.

### It's not a medical diet

Fresh food is a great everyday option, but it isn't a substitute for a prescription diet. Dogs with conditions like pancreatitis, certain heart conditions, kidney disease, or specific protein allergies may need a formula tailored to them. If your dog has a diagnosed health issue, talk to your vet before switching, and see our overview of [dog food for food allergies](/blog/dog-food-for-food-allergies) if sensitivities are the concern.

## Who Freshpet Fits Best

Fresh refrigerated food tends to be worth it for:

- **Picky eaters** who turn their noses up at kibble — the palatability is a real selling point.
- **Dogs who need more moisture** in their diet, including some seniors.
- **Owners who want minimally processed food** and are comfortable with the cost and fridge storage.

It's a tougher sell for very large dogs (the cost), households without fridge space, or owners who need grab-and-go convenience. In those cases, a high-quality kibble or a fresh-as-topper approach often makes more sense. If you're weighing moisture-rich options in general, our guide to [wet dog food](/blog/wet-dog-food) covers similar ground, and [senior dog food](/blog/senior-dog-food) digs into what changes as dogs age.

## How to Choose a Recipe and Make the Switch

Before buying, check the package's **nutritional adequacy statement**. Choose a meal labeled complete and balanced for your dog's life stage; a treat or mixer is not a full diet. Puppies need a recipe labeled for growth or all life stages. For a large-breed puppy, check that the statement also covers the growth of large-size dogs. The [WSAVA's nutrition guidance](https://wsava.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Frequently-Asked-Questions-and-Myths.pdf) explains what this statement can tell you.

Use the feeding chart on the specific recipe as a starting point and adjust with your vet for your dog's age, body condition, and activity. Our guide to [how much food to feed your dog](/blog/how-much-food-should-i-feed-my-dog) explains the portion basics. For a broader comparison before you buy, see our [fresh dog food guide](/blog/fresh-dog-food).

If you decide to switch, [Freshpet recommends a gradual 7–10 day transition](https://www.freshpet.com/contact): mix a small amount with your dog's current food, then increase the new food over several days. Slow down and ask your vet if your dog develops persistent digestive upset.

## Practical Takeaways

- **A Freshpet meal can be a good everyday food** when its label says complete and balanced for your dog's life stage. Treats and mixers are not full diets.
- **Fresh isn't automatically better than kibble.** A well-formulated food from a reputable company matters more than the format. Good kibble is still a solid choice.
- **The practical advantages are moisture and palatability** — useful for some picky eaters and dogs whose vet recommends a moisture-rich diet.
- **The trade-offs are cost, fridge storage, safe handling, and a short open shelf life.** Check the package because storage windows vary. Using fresh as a topper is a common middle ground.
- **It's not a medical diet.** Dogs with health conditions should get a vet's input before switching.

If you've been curious about the fridge-case food, the takeaway is reassuring: it's real nutrition, not a gimmick. Whether it's right for _your_ dog comes down to your dog's needs, your budget, and how much fridge space you're willing to give up.
