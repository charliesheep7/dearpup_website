Separation anxiety is one of the hardest things to live with as a dog owner. It isn't naughtiness or spite — it's panic, and a panicking dog can't learn its way out on willpower. By the time most people start researching medication, they've already tried the crate, the long walk before work, the enrichment toy, and the camera that shows the howling starts ninety seconds after the door closes.

Medication is a legitimate tool here. It's also widely misunderstood. Here's what it does, what it doesn't, and what to ask your vet.

## The Short Answer

**Two medications are FDA-approved in the US specifically for canine separation anxiety: fluoxetine (Reconcile) and clomipramine (Clomicalm).** Both are approved for use *in conjunction with* a behavior modification plan — that pairing is part of the label, not a footnote.

Your vet may also prescribe other drugs off-label, such as trazodone for situational anxiety, depending on your dog's pattern. Off-label prescribing is normal and legal in veterinary medicine.

One thing to be clear about up front: none of this is something to source yourself. Human antidepressants in a household medicine cabinet are a common cause of accidental poisoning in dogs, and dosing is not a matter of scaling by body weight.

## The Two Approved Options

### Fluoxetine (Reconcile)

Fluoxetine is an SSRI — the same drug class as Prozac in humans. [VCA Animal Hospitals](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/fluoxetine) and the [AKC](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/health/prozac-fluoxetine-for-dogs/) both describe its use for separation anxiety, generalized anxiety, compulsive behaviors, and some forms of aggression.

It comes as a chewable tablet given once daily.

**The timeline matters most.** As the AKC puts it, the drug won't "affect how they're behaving until we have an effective blood level which could take two to four weeks." Many owners conclude it failed at day ten. It hadn't started yet.

### Clomipramine (Clomicalm)

Clomipramine is a tricyclic antidepressant, approved for dogs older than six months. It works through a different pathway than fluoxetine but arrives at a similar place. Head-to-head comparisons suggest both are **similarly effective** at reducing fear and anxiety symptoms.

Its timeline is even longer: it generally takes **up to two months** to judge whether clomipramine is helping, alongside the behavior work.

Which one your vet picks usually comes down to your dog's other health conditions, side effect profile, and what's worked before.

## Side Effects Worth Knowing

The most common side effects of fluoxetine, per the AKC, are **appetite loss and drowsiness**. Also reported: vomiting, diarrhea, trembling, restlessness, excessive drooling, and weight loss. Severe reactions such as seizures or persistent vomiting are uncommon but need a same-day call to your vet.

A frustrating quirk of the timeline: side effects often appear within a day or two, while benefits take weeks. The hardest stretch is usually the first fortnight, when you're seeing costs without returns. Tell your vet what you're seeing rather than quietly stopping.

Two safety points the AKC flags:

- **Never stop abruptly.** Fluoxetine requires gradual weaning under vet supervision.
- **Drug interactions are real.** Combining fluoxetine with certain medications — trazodone or clomipramine among them — can cause serotonin syndrome. Tell your vet about every medication and supplement your dog takes, including over-the-counter calming products.

## Medication Is Half the Treatment

This is the part people skip, so it's worth being direct.

The AKC states that fluoxetine "absolutely has to be coupled with behavior modification to train these dogs not to do the behavior that we want to stop." That isn't legal hedging. Medication lowers your dog's baseline panic enough that learning becomes possible. It doesn't do the teaching.

The behavior side generally involves:

1. **Graduated departures.** Starting with absences so short the dog doesn't react — sometimes seconds — and extending slowly enough that panic never triggers.
2. **Breaking the pre-departure cues.** Picking up keys, putting on shoes, and the door routine become alarm bells. Doing them at random times without leaving defuses them.
3. **Keeping arrivals and departures boring.** Emotional goodbyes raise the stakes of the gap.
4. **Not leaving them longer than they can handle** during treatment, which for some households means daycare, a sitter, or a neighbor for a while.

A veterinary behaviorist or a trainer experienced with separation anxiety is worth the money here. This is a condition where generic obedience advice doesn't transfer, and where going too fast sets you back weeks.

## When to Talk to Your Vet

Book the conversation if your dog, specifically when left alone:

- Howls, barks, or whines persistently
- Destroys things near exits — doors, windows, crate bars — often injuring themselves
- Urinates or defecates indoors despite being reliably house-trained
- Paces, drools heavily, or refuses food the moment you leave
- Panics as you go through your leaving routine, before you've left

Two things to bring: **phone video of your dog alone**, which is the single most useful piece of evidence a vet can have, and a rough log of how often and how long it happens.

Also worth ruling out: some of these signs overlap with medical problems. A dog urinating indoors may have a urinary tract issue; a dog suddenly clingy and vocal may be in pain. Our guide to [telling if your dog is sick](/blog/how-can-you-tell-if-your-dog-is-sick) covers what else to consider.

## Practical Takeaways

Medication for separation anxiety isn't a shortcut or an admission of failure — it's often what makes the training possible for a dog whose panic response is too strong to work through otherwise.

Set expectations accordingly: two to four weeks for fluoxetine, up to two months for clomipramine, both paired with consistent behavior work, and neither stopped without a vet-supervised taper. Most dogs improve substantially. Many eventually come off medication. Very few get there in a fortnight.
