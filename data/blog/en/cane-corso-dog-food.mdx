The Cane Corso is a big, powerful working dog with a big appetite to match. Feeding one well isn't complicated, but because of the breed's size and build, a few feeding habits matter more than they would for a smaller dog.

Here's how to choose a good food for your Cane Corso, how often to feed, and the simple steps that help keep mealtimes safe.

## Good Dog Food for Cane Corso — The Short Answer

**Feed a high-quality, complete and balanced food made for your dog's life stage, split into smaller meals, and keep exercise away from mealtimes.** You don't need a special "Cane Corso" formula.

The [AKC](https://www.akc.org/dog-breeds/cane-corso/) says the breed should do well on a high-quality dog food, whether commercial or home-prepared with your vet's supervision and approval. [PetMD](https://www.petmd.com/dog/breeds/cane-corso) adds that it's highly recommended that all dogs eat a diet meeting AAFCO nutritional standards.

## What Makes Feeding a Cane Corso Different

### A giant, deep-chested body

PetMD describes Cane Corsos as dogs that can weigh over 100 pounds and stand 28 inches tall at the shoulder. The AKC lists heights of 25 to 27.5 inches for males and 23.5 to 26 inches for females, with weight in proportion to height.

That size and deep chest put the breed at risk for **bloat**. PetMD explains that large, deep-chested breeds are susceptible to gastric dilatation-volvulus (GDV), a life-threatening condition where the stomach fills with gas and can twist on itself. This is the main reason the way you feed a Cane Corso matters as much as what you feed.

### Joints and weight

PetMD lists obesity, hip and elbow dysplasia, epilepsy, and bloat among the issues the breed can be prone to. The AKC also notes that some dogs are prone to getting overweight and that too many treats can cause obesity. Keeping a big dog lean takes pressure off their joints.

### Lifespan

The AKC lists a life expectancy of 9 to 12 years, and PetMD notes that's a little longer than average for a giant breed. A large UK study found a lower median of 8.1 years for the breed. You can read more in our guide to the [shortest dog life expectancy](/blog/shortest-dog-life-expectancy). Good feeding habits and a healthy weight are some of the most practical ways to support a long, active life.

## How to Choose a Food for Your Cane Corso

You can tune out most of the marketing on the bag. Focus on these points.

- **An AAFCO "complete and balanced" statement** for your dog's life stage: puppy, adult, or senior.
- **A food made for big dogs.** Large-breed formulas are designed with bigger bodies in mind. Our guide to [large breed dog food](/blog/large-breed-dog-food) explains what to look for.
- **A calorie count that fits your dog.** Every bag lists calories per cup. A very active working dog and a laid-back house dog can need quite different amounts.
- **A food your dog does well on.** Good stools, steady energy, and a healthy coat are good signs. If you switch, go slowly. Our guide to [changing dog food](/blog/changing-dog-food) has a day-by-day plan.

### Feeding a Cane Corso puppy

Giant-breed puppies grow fast, and the right puppy food matters. Ask your vet whether a large-breed puppy formula suits your dog, and see our guide to [puppy food for large breed puppies](/blog/puppy-food-for-large-breed-puppies) for more detail.

## How to Feed a Cane Corso Safely

PetMD recommends a few precautions because of the breed's bloat risk:

1. **Feed smaller meals throughout the day** instead of one big meal.
2. **Avoid elevated food bowls.**
3. **Prevent exercise just before or after eating.** Plan walks and play so they don't fall right around mealtime.

For meal frequency, PetMD says **puppies should eat three to four times a day** on a regular schedule, and **adults at least twice a day**. Our guide to [how many times a day a dog should eat](/blog/how-many-times-a-day-should-a-dog-eat) covers schedules in more detail.

### How much to feed

There's no one-size-fits-all portion. PetMD explains that it depends on the food's calorie density and your dog's energy needs, and recommends asking your vet for portion sizes. Start with the feeding guide on the bag, measure every meal, and adjust as needed. Our [dog calorie calculator](/blog/dog-calorie-calculator) can give you a starting estimate.

## Do Cane Corsos Need Supplements?

PetMD notes that dogs on a well-balanced, AAFCO-approved diet don't need extra vitamin and mineral supplements. Some supplements, such as joint support products or omega-3 fatty acids, may be helpful for certain dogs. Talk with your vet before adding anything, especially for a growing puppy. Our [dog supplements guide](/blog/dog-supplements) explains when they can help.

## Know the Signs of Bloat

Because bloat can happen suddenly, every Cane Corso owner should know what to watch for. The AKC says owners should learn the signs and what to do if they occur. PetMD advises contacting an emergency veterinarian immediately if you suspect bloat or GDV. PetMD lists symptoms including a stomach that enlarges quickly, retching without bringing anything up, and signs of belly pain such as whining, reluctance to walk, or not eating. If you see these, don't wait. Get your dog seen right away.

## Practical Takeaways

- Choose a complete and balanced food that meets AAFCO standards for your dog's life stage.
- Feed puppies three to four times a day and adults at least twice a day.
- Split food into smaller meals, skip elevated bowls, and keep exercise away from mealtimes.
- Measure portions and count treats to keep your dog lean.
- Ask your vet about portion sizes and before adding supplements.
