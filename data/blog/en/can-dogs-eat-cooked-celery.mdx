## The Short Answer

**Yes, dogs can eat cooked celery, as long as it's plain.** No onion, no garlic, no salt, no butter. Steamed or boiled celery, cut into small pieces, makes a fine low-calorie treat.

The catch is where the cooked celery comes from. A bit of plain steamed stalk is one thing. Celery fished out of your soup, stew or stuffing is another, and it's usually best kept off your dog's plate.

## Is Cooked Celery Safe for Dogs?

According to [PetMD](https://petmd.com/dog/nutrition/can-dogs-eat-celery), in an article reviewed by veterinarian Dr. Sandra C. Mitchell, cooked celery can be fine for dogs **as long as you prepare it completely plain**. The [AKC](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/nutrition/can-dogs-eat-celery/) agrees that celery is generally safe for dogs and notes it can be a good option for dogs that need to lose weight.

Celery itself isn't toxic. PetMD says feeding too much won't poison your dog, but too much of anything can still cause an upset stomach.

### Why plain matters

Celery rarely gets cooked on its own in a human kitchen. It shows up in soups, stocks, casseroles and stir-fries, usually with onion, garlic and plenty of salt. PetMD points out that **onion and garlic are toxic to dogs**, so any celery you cook for your dog needs to skip both. Our list of [toxic foods for dogs](/blog/toxic-foods-for-dogs) covers other common kitchen ingredients to watch.

## What's in Celery

Celery is mostly water and crunch, but it carries more than you might think. The AKC lists these nutrients:

- Fiber
- Vitamins A, C and K
- Folate
- Potassium
- Manganese

PetMD adds calcium and antioxidants to the list, and notes that celery's fiber can help dogs feel full and support digestive health. The AKC also describes celery as low in fat and cholesterol, and says its crunch may help freshen your dog's breath.

One thing to know: PetMD says **frozen celery loses most of its nutrients** in the freezing process. It's still safe, just less useful. Fresh or plain cooked celery keeps more of the good stuff.

## Raw vs. Cooked Celery

Both work. The AKC says raw celery is safe if it's washed and cut into bite-size pieces. Cooking just changes the texture.

- **Raw celery** is crunchy and hydrating, and it gives your dog something to chew.
- **Cooked celery** is softer, which can help older dogs, dogs with dental issues or dogs who are wary of new textures.

Whichever you choose, cut it small. PetMD warns that a whole celery stick could become a choking hazard or cause an intestinal blockage, and recommends removing the stringy fibers too.

## How to Prepare Cooked Celery for Your Dog

1. **Wash the stalks** well under running water.
2. **Remove the leaves** unless the celery is organic. PetMD notes the leaves are where most pesticides are found.
3. **Pull off the strings** and cut the stalk into small pieces sized for your dog.
4. **Steam or boil in plain water.** Skip oil, butter, salt, stock and seasoning.
5. **Cool completely** before serving.

Want to add a little extra appeal? PetMD suggests a dab of **sugar-free, xylitol-free and preferably salt-free peanut butter** on the celery. Check the label every time, since xylitol is dangerous for dogs.

## How Much Celery to Give

Celery is a treat, not a meal. The AKC says treats should make up **no more than 10% of your dog's daily calories**. If you're not sure what that is for your dog, our [dog calorie calculator](/blog/dog-calorie-calculator) can help.

PetMD offers this rough guide by size:

| Dog size | Weight | Suggested amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Extra-small | 2 to 20 lb | 1 to 2 small pieces |
| Small | 21 to 30 lb | 2 to 3 pieces |
| Medium | 31 to 50 lb | 5 to 6 pieces |
| Large | 51 to 90 lb | A small handful |
| Extra-large | 91 lb and up | A handful |

The first time you offer celery, start with less than this. The AKC notes that some dogs get mild digestive upset when trying celery for the first time.

### Puppies

The AKC says puppies can eat small amounts of celery if it's cut into suitably small pieces. Supervise closely, because choking is the main risk.

## When to Skip Celery

Celery is a safe snack for most dogs, but check with your vet first if your dog:

- Is on a prescription or therapeutic diet
- Has a sensitive stomach or ongoing digestive issues
- Tends to gulp food without chewing

PetMD recommends checking with your vet before adding any new people food, especially for dogs on a special diet.

## Practical Takeaways

- Plain cooked celery is safe for dogs. Seasoned celery from human dishes is not a good idea.
- Never cook it with onion or garlic, which are toxic to dogs.
- Cut it small and remove the strings to prevent choking.
- Keep celery and other treats under 10% of daily calories.
- Start with a small amount and watch for an upset stomach.
