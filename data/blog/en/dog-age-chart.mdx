## How to Use This Canine Age Chart

**The quick version: a 1-year-old dog is about 15 in human years, a 2-year-old is about 24, and after that each birthday adds roughly 4 to 6 human years depending on size.**

The old "multiply by seven" rule is easy to remember, but it gets both ends wrong. It makes young dogs look far too young and large senior dogs look younger than they really are. The chart below fixes that by splitting dogs into size groups.

Find your dog's age on the left, then read across to their size column. If you want the reasoning behind the numbers, our longer explainer on [dog years to human years](/blog/dog-years) walks through the science.

## Dog Age Chart by Size

The first two years come from the American Veterinary Medical Association guideline, as summarized by the [AKC](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/health/how-to-calculate-dog-years-to-human-years/): about 15 human years for year one and about 9 more for year two. After that, the AVMA figure is about 5 human years per dog year for a medium dog.

We adjusted that for size: roughly 4 years per birthday for small dogs and 6 for large and giant dogs. That reflects the pattern [VCA Animal Hospitals](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/how-old-is-old-comparing-dog-age-to-human-age) describes, where smaller dogs age more slowly and live longer while bigger dogs age faster. **Treat every number here as an estimate, not a diagnosis.**

| -------- | ------------------- | ----------------- | ---------------------- |
| 1 year   | 15                  | 15                | 15                     |
| 2 years  | 24                  | 24                | 24                     |
| 3 years  | 28                  | 29                | 30                     |
| 4 years  | 32                  | 34                | 36                     |
| 5 years  | 36                  | 39                | 42                     |
| 6 years  | 40                  | 44                | 48                     |
| 7 years  | 44                  | 49                | 54                     |
| 8 years  | 48                  | 54                | 60                     |
| 9 years  | 52                  | 59                | 66                     |
| 10 years | 56                  | 64                | 72                     |
| 11 years | 60                  | 69                | 78                     |
| 12 years | 64                  | 74                | 84                     |
| 13 years | 68                  | 79                | 90                     |
| 14 years | 72                  | 84                | —                      |
| 15 years | 76                  | 89                | —                      |

Few large or giant dogs reach 14, which is why those cells are blank. For a closer look at a specific birthday, see our guides to [2 years in dog years](/blog/2-years-in-dog-years), [9 years in dog years](/blog/9-years-in-dog-years), and [12 years in dog years](/blog/12-years-in-dog-years).

## The DNA-Based Dog Age Formula

In 2020, researchers published a different approach in the journal _Cell Systems_. According to the [National Human Genome Research Institute](https://www.genome.gov/news/news-release/NHGRI-researchers-reframe-dog-to-human-aging-comparisons), the team compared DNA methylation patterns (chemical tags that build up on DNA with age) in 104 dogs, mostly Labrador Retrievers, and 320 people.

Their formula is: **human age = 16 × ln(dog age) + 31**, where "ln" is the natural logarithm.

Here's what that formula gives for common ages:

| Dog age  | Human age (DNA formula) |
| -------- | ----------------------- |
| 1 year   | 31                      |
| 2 years  | 42                      |
| 4 years  | 53                      |
| 6 years  | 60                      |
| 8 years  | 64                      |
| 10 years | 68                      |
| 12 years | 71                      |
| 14 years | 73                      |

The researchers found that an 8-week-old puppy lines up with a 9-month-old baby, and a 12-year-old Labrador lines up with a 70-year-old person.

### Which chart should you trust?

Both are useful for different things. The DNA formula captures how fast dogs race through early life, but it was built mostly on one large breed. The AKC notes that it may not fit every dog, because breeds age differently.

For everyday decisions, the size chart is the more practical guide. It lines up better with when vets actually change their care advice.

## When Does a Dog Become a Senior?

Size matters most here. According to VCA Animal Hospitals:

- **Small dogs:** senior at around 11 to 12 years
- **Medium dogs:** senior at about 10 years
- **Large dogs:** senior at about 8 years
- **Giant breeds:** senior at around 7 years

That means a 9-year-old Chihuahua and a 9-year-old Great Dane are at very different stages. The Chihuahua is middle-aged. The Great Dane is well into its senior years.

## What the Chart Means for Daily Care

A dog age chart is more than trivia. It helps you see how quickly things change.

**One calendar year is several human years for your dog.** Skipping an annual checkup is a bit like a person going four or five years without seeing a doctor. That's why many vets suggest checkups twice a year once a dog reaches senior age.

**Small shifts in habits add up fast.** Extra weight, skipped tooth brushing, or too little exercise compound at dog speed. The upside is that good habits pay off quickly too.

**Watch for early aging signs.** Stiffness after naps, more sleep, cloudy eyes, and changes in appetite often show up first. The [VCA guide to senior dog care](https://www.vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/senior-dog-care) is a helpful checklist for what to raise with your vet.

**Plan for your dog's likely lifespan.** Size and breed shape how long most dogs live. Our guides to [how long dogs live](/blog/how-long-do-dogs-live) and [how long small dogs live](/blog/how-long-do-small-dogs-live) give realistic ranges.

## Practical Takeaways

- A 1-year-old dog is about 15 in human years, and a 2-year-old is about 24
- After year two, add about 4 years per birthday for small dogs, 5 for medium, and 6 for large and giant dogs
- The DNA-based formula (16 × ln(age) + 31) shows how fast puppies mature, but was built mostly on Labradors
- Senior age ranges from about 7 for giant breeds to 11 or 12 for small breeds
- Use your dog's "human age" as a reminder to book checkups and adjust food and exercise on time
